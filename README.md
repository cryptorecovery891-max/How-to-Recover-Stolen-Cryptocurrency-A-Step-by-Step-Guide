Act Quickly and Secure Remaining Assets
Time is critical after a crypto theft. The longer stolen funds remain untraced, the more opportunities scammers have to move them through mixers, multiple wallets, or exchanges.
First, secure any remaining cryptocurrency and accounts. Change passwords, enable two-factor authentication on every exchange and wallet, and move untouched funds to a secure cold wallet if possible. Do not send additional funds to anyone claiming they can “unlock” or “verify” your account—this is a common secondary scam.
Document everything immediately: transaction IDs, wallet addresses involved, screenshots of communications, dates, and any suspicious emails or messages. This evidence becomes essential for both law-enforcement reports and professional recovery efforts.
Report the Theft to Relevant Parties
Report the incident to the exchange or platform involved (if applicable), your local law-enforcement agency, and, where relevant, national cybercrime units. Many exchanges maintain specialized fraud or recovery teams and can freeze funds still under their control.
Filing a police report creates an official record that legitimate crypto recovery companies and blockchain investigators can reference. Keep copies of all confirmation numbers and correspondence. While reporting alone rarely returns funds, it strengthens your case when you later engage professional help.

Understand the Blockchain Forensics Process
Professional crypto recovery relies on blockchain analysis rather than guesswork. Investigators trace the movement of stolen assets across public ledgers, identify patterns, and work to locate funds that may still be accessible on exchanges or in identifiable wallets.
The process typically includes:

Mapping the transaction trail from the original theft
Identifying intermediary addresses and potential exit points
Coordinating with exchanges and, when appropriate, law enforcement
Preparing documentation for any legal or recovery actions
